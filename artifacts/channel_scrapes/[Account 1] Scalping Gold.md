**Traders** **Mistake: Focusing on short-term results**

The average trader usually understands that results should be evaluated over the long term, but in practice, after each trade, they look at their balance and immediately jump to conclusions. One losing position, and they panic. One quick win, and it seems like the system is perfect.

**▸ **The mistake is that the trader begins to perceive the market through the prism of individual trades rather than the system. As a result, they are either happy or stressed, and all this prevents them from seeing the big picture.

**➡️**** Why this is dangerous:**

1. Constant emotional swings lead to chaotic actions.
2. Discipline is disrupted: the trader begins to jump between strategies.
3. There are no real statistics.

The trader enters a trade, loses 25 points, and immediately decides that “this strategy doesn't work.” He abandons it, tries another one, gets a random win, and thinks, “That's it, I've found my system.” A week later, the result is complete chaos because none of the methods have been followed through to the end.

**🟢**** How to avoid mistakes:**

1. Keep a trading journal and record results not by trade, but by week or month.
2. Set a goal to “work out the system over time.”
3. Evaluate progress on at least 20-30 trades.
4. Separate the psychology of trading: one losing trade does not mean a bad strategy, and one winning trade does not mean a perfect one.
5. Set reporting periods for yourself (for example, summarize the week every Sunday and do a full review at the end of the month).

A professional always thinks in terms of series. They understand that one trade does not decide anything. Only distance matters. It is what provides stable growth.
